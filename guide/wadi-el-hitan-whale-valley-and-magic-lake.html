Wadi El Hitan and Magic Lake: What to See

5 min read

Fayoum is the oasis south-west of Cairo, and its desert side holds two of the most unusual stops in Egypt: a valley of fossil whales and a quiet lake among the dunes. Here is what you will see and how to plan the day.

Quick answer: Wadi El Hitan (Whale Valley) is a UNESCO World Heritage site in the Wadi El Rayan protected area, famous for fossil whales about 40 million years old. Magic Lake is a short drive away among the dunes. Together they make a full day from Cairo of about 9 hours, with hotel pickup and a private driver.

What Is Wadi El Hitan?

Wadi El Hitan means Valley of the Whales. Millions of years ago this area was under the sea, and the desert floor now holds the skeletons of early whales, along with other sea life. It was named a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2005, and it is one of the best places in the world to see how whales evolved from land animals.

What You Will See There

  • Whale skeletons: fossils lie in the open, along a marked walking trail, so you can see them where they were found.
  • Fossil shells and sea life: traces of the ancient sea are visible in the rock around you.
  • Desert views: wide, quiet scenery, very different from the busy streets of Cairo.
  • Site museum: a small exhibit that explains the fossils before or after your walk.

Magic Lake and Wadi El Rayan

Wadi El Rayan is a protected area with lakes, waterfalls and dunes. Magic Lake is a calm stretch of water set among sand, a relaxing stop after the fossil trail. It is a good place for photos and a break before the drive back.

How Long Is the Trip From Cairo?

The drive is a few hours each way, and the whole day takes about 9 hours with stops. Start early so you have time at both places and return before the evening traffic.

Best Time to Go

The cooler months, from October to April, are the most comfortable for walking in the desert. In summer the middle of the day is very hot, so start early and bring plenty of water.

Tips for the Day

  • Wear comfortable closed shoes, as the trail is sandy and uneven.
  • Bring sunglasses, a hat and sunscreen, as there is little shade.
  • Carry a little cash in Egyptian pounds for small purchases and site fees.
  • Charge your phone and camera, as the scenery is very photogenic.

Frequently Asked Questions

A UNESCO World Heritage site in Fayoum, also called Whale Valley, where fossils of early whales about 40 million years old are visible in the desert.

Yes, if you enjoy nature, geology or quiet places away from the crowds. It is a very different experience from the pyramids and museums.

From October to April, when the weather is cooler. In summer the heat is strong in the middle of the day.
